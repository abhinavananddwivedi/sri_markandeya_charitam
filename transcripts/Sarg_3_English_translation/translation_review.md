# Sarga 3 — translation quality review

Scope: canto 3 (manuscript pages 61–86, ślokas 1–55), assessed as two separate layers —
Sanskrit → Hindi (the amanuensis `translation` and grandfather's `notes`) and
Hindi → English (`translation_en`, `notes_en`, and on pages 76–86 the added literal-meaning,
metre, and variant-reading apparatus). Reviewed against the manuscript scans and the paired
Devanagari transcription. Not published to the site.

## Overall grades

| Layer | Grade | One-line justification |
|---|---|---|
| Sanskrit → Hindi | **A−** | Faithful, compact `translation`; expansive but on-topic `notes`. Pages 76–86 are unusually transparent about where the amanuensis's hand is corrupt. The one weak spot (the 53–55 benedictory triad) is deliberately left unglossed. |
| Hindi → English | **A− / A** | Accurate, disciplined (stays inside the Hindi, invents nothing), consistent IAST and proper nouns, technical terms glossed inline. Pages 76–86 add genuine scholarly value (separate literal vs. commentarial renderings, metre, paṭhabheda). Minor, source-faithful over-adjectivisation; a few "manuscript notes" drift from paleography into plot summary. |

## Systematic observations

- **One-to-one fidelity.** Every `translation_en` tracks its Hindi `translation`; every `notes_en`
  tracks its Hindi `notes`. No verse where the English drifts from, contradicts, or outruns the
  Hindi it renders.
- **Proper nouns and IAST are consistent** across all 26 pages: Mṛkaṇḍu, Parāśara, Gaṅgā,
  Keśava, Dardura, Naimiṣāraṇya, Ayodhyā, Gomatī, Mudgala, Bhṛgu, Dhātā, Vivasvat / Āditya.
- **Inline glossing is a real asset**: madhuparka, agnihotra, pravṛtti-mārga, ūrdhvapuṇḍra,
  somapāyī, parṇa-kuṭī, sūryopasthāna, trikālajñatā, lagna, vācaka are each italicised and
  explained at first use.
- **Register runs ornate** ("profound serenity", "supremely auspicious", "intensely radiant
  Divine Light"). This is faithful — grandfather's Hindi is itself ornate ("परम समुज्ज्वल
  सुदीप्त ज्योति", "अत्यन्त प्रसन्न मुदितचित्त") — not English invention, but a reader wanting a
  plainer style should know it is there.
- **Pages 76–86 are the most scholarly section of the whole corpus.** They separate a literal
  verse translation (IV) from grandfather's commentarial expansion (V), identify the metre
  (Rathoddhatā) with scansion on every śloka, and record the amanuensis's actual (often broken)
  readings against the reconstructed text. Cantos 1–2 have no equivalent apparatus.
- **Minor: category slippage in a few dialect-B `manuscript_note`s.** Most are true paleography
  (p76, p79, p82). A few are plot summary instead — e.g. p86 v52's note is entirely narrative
  ("Having received the blessing, cow, companion sage…"), with no variant reading. Harmless, but
  not what the field name implies.
- **Weakest underlying text: the 53–55 triad on p86.** The dialect-B Sanskrit of these verses
  (`मग्न्यन्तेन हि शितस्य सुशीलकेण`, `विद्बुद्धजनानर्चनवता`) is visibly more corrupt than the
  parallel benedictory verses transcribed on canto-1 p032 and canto-2 p060
  (`विद्वज्जनार्चनरता धरणीधरेण`). Because the manuscript itself defers the gloss of this triad
  to p32, nothing turns on it here — but if these verses are ever presented in full, prefer the
  p032 / p060 readings.

## What could not be verified

- The English is a translation **of the Hindi**, not an independent re-derivation from the
  Sanskrit (project convention). Where the Hindi itself misreads a corrupt śloka, the English
  faithfully carries that misreading; this review checked Hindi→English fidelity and did
  Sanskrit→Hindi plausibility spot-checks, not a full re-edition of the Sanskrit.
- Metrical scansion on pages 76–86 was sanity-checked against the Rathoddhatā template
  (– ⏑ – | ⏑ ⏑ ⏑ | – ⏑ – | ⏑ –) for a sample, not verified pāda-by-pāda for all 24 verses.
- Traditional equivalences given in the notes (e.g. yojana ≈ 24 miles) are reasonable but not
  authoritative.
