Verse 49

Metre: Svāgatā. Pāda 1 has 10 syllables (as written; a syllable seems to be missing). The first half of pāda 3 is unclear (Hindi: "by royal attendants, in carts"). English NotesAI-assisted translation
On the date of the betrothal (rājtilak) fixed beforehand by the assembly of learned men, he gathered all the things for the varaṇa and, in a royal and honourable manner (as if some very wealthy person were being chosen), loaded them on several bullock carts and sent them with Sage Dākṣi (who was also his associate and family priest), together with many young brahmacārīs. Verse 50
Shlokamanuensis's hand
तत्र खारिपचनं शुचि शुभ्रं कांस्यवर्तुलयुगं सुरुचे द्राक् । किञ्च चन्द्ररुचिकंसयुगेन स्थालराजमपि राजतभामित् ॥ ५० ॥
Manuscript NoteAI-assisted transcription
वृत्त: स्वागता। पाद १ में मूलतः "क्षीरपचनं" लिखा था; "क्षीर" रेखांकित है और ऊपर "खारिं" लिखा है (संशोधित पाठ "खारिपचनं")। लिपिकार का अनुवाद मूल पाठ "क्षीर" (दूध पकाने) का अनुसरण करता है; रचनाकार की टिप्पणी संशोधित "खारीपचन" का। पाद ४ "राजतभामित्" (पूर्व निष्कासन का "रजत" भ्रान्त)।
Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Svāgatā. Pāda 1 originally read "क्षीरपचनं"; "क्षीर" is underlined with "खारिं" written above, giving the corrected reading "खारिपचनं". The amanuensis's Hindi follows the original "क्षीर" ("for boiling milk"); the poet's notes follow the corrected "खारीपचन". Pāda 4 reads "राजतभामित्"; the earlier export's "रजत" was wrong.
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
There a pure, bright khārī-pacana (great cooking vessel) and a pair of round bronze pots shone at once; and, with a pair of moon-bright bronze vessels, a splendid platter shining like silver. (50)

English Translation (of the Hindi)AI-assisted translation
Among those provisions, a round bronze pot (baṭlohī) for (boiling) milk looked handsome, and with two water-serving jugs bright as the moon there was also a large platter (parāt) made of silver. (50)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
Among those provisions was a large vessel, a khārīpacan (in which 32 or 64 ser of rice can be cooked), very bright and pure. With it, two bronze pots (baṭulohiyā) also spread their gleam, and two large water-serving vessels made of bronze and brass outshone even the radiance of the moon. Along with these there was also a very large, gleaming platter of phūl (bell-metal). (50)
