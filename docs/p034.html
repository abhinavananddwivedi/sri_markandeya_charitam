Likewise, the pair floating in the middle of the water filled him with wonder as they sank and rose; he reflected that these [are] people in the ocean of worldly life, and on [their] birth and death too. (3)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
The two kept sinking and rising in the very bright waters of the goddess Gaṅgā in the course of their natural water play. Seeing this, the sage Mṛkaṇḍu was very amazed, and he also reflected on the worldly birth and death of people who keep diving, sinking and rising in this wheel of worldly life. (3)

Somewhere he saw a heron with closed eyes, black inside and white outside. Then he concluded that hypocritical people, firmly set on accomplishing their own ends under a pretext, are just like this. (4)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
He also saw a row of herons, by their nature with eyes closed in one place and open in another, impure of heart, ever alert to their target, outwardly bearing very white bodies but inwardly very eager and violent toward fish. Seeing them, the idea came into his mind how much hypocrisy this world creates to gain its ends and how firmly it stays engaged in serving itself; so he thought in his mind. (4)
