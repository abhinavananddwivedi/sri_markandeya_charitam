English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Reaching Puṣkaradvīpa, [land] of people who live ten thousand years, he found no contentment; nor did he think Krauñca, adorned with forests and ten thousand mountains, good for himself. (21)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
First he went and inspected Puṣkaradvīpa, where people's lifespan is ten thousand years, but the place did not suit his mind. From there he went to Krauñcadvīpa and looked over its thousands of mountains, valleys and many lovely forests too, considering them as places to live; but that dvīpa too did not seem suitable for building his dwelling. Moved by the fear of being pierced and wounded by the tips of kuśa grass, he considered Kuśadvīpa inauspicious. Hearing that in Yama's abode there are thorny śālmali (silk-cotton) trees, the land of Śālmalidvīpa too gave him no joy. (22)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
Inspecting the various dvīpas, the sage Mṛkaṇḍu reached Kuśadvīpa. Seeing land thick with kuśa grass there, he feared that its sharp tips would pierce and wound him, and considered Kuśadvīpa inauspicious too. Then, hearing in talk of the śālmali tree in Yama's abode that the silk-cotton tree is very thorny, he did not find Śālmalidvīpa desirable or pleasing either; he considered it inauspicious and left it. (22)
