Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Svāgatā (in pāda 1 and pādas 3–4 the manuscript text does not fully fit the metre; it is kept as written). Pāda 1 reads "सर्वमेव वरणीय[मुदीर्य] पाठैः", pāda 2 "मङ्गलायनमुदीर्य शुभं द्राक्", pāda 3 "कुम्भलग्नमपरो करतल्ले", and pāda 4 "सम्मदेन पुरतो वितताम", confirmed by the Hindi ("having recited the auspicious blessing, ... put forward to give into the groom's hand to touch the kumbha"). Some letters in pādas 1 and 3 are unclear. The earlier export's "मङ्गलैः", "मथाऽथ कुम्भयुक्", "कराग्रके" and "वितस्तरे" are not in the manuscript.
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Having recited with readings all that belongs to the betrothal, and quickly uttered the auspicious blessing, they joyfully held out before him, into his palm, the things that had touched the kumbha. (59)

Manuscript NoteAI-assisted transcription
वृत्त: स्वागता (पाद १ "कान्तिकारि" में एक लघु वर्ण अधिक प्रतीत होता है)। पाद २ "स्त्वञ्जलौ विनिहिताः कनमुद्राः" ("कन" = कनक, हस्तलेख के अनुसार)। पाद ४ का पहला अक्षर अधिलिखित है; पाठ "नापुरीक्षणयुगानि" (अनुवाद "नेत्रयुगल ... नहीं पा सके" से पुष्ट; पूर्व निष्कासन का "नापुरीक्षित" भ्रान्त था)।
Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Svāgatā (pāda 1 "कान्तिकारि" seems to have one extra light syllable). Pāda 2 reads "स्त्वञ्जलौ विनिहिताः कनमुद्राः" ("कन" = kanaka, as written). The first letter of pāda 4 is overwritten; the reading is "नापुरीक्षणयुगानि", confirmed by the Hindi ("the pairs of eyes ... could not find"); the earlier export's "नापुरीक्षित" was wrong.
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
A thousand gleaming gold coins were placed in his cupped hands; amid the heap of yellow rice, the pairs of eyes of the people could not tell them apart. (60)

A thousand gold coins were placed in the groom's cupped hands; among the heap of yellow akṣata, people's pairs of eyes could not find any difference. (60)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
At that moment a thousand gold coins (exceedingly bright) were placed in the groom's hands. The joyful gaze of the onlookers could not pay any particular attention to the yellow rice akṣata lying alongside, because all around it was the radiance of the gold coins that was so captivating. (60)
