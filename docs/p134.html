Their heads bound with crown-like turbans, in bright, shining coats, clear and fair as Smara (Kāma), the men riding on them too, having struck up friendships, chatted with one another, smiling. (31)

विरलमालिषु वाजिगजादिषु छविरियं परिदृश्यत एव यत् । किमु सुसज्जितराजिगता तदा श्वशुरसद्ममुखी सुषमा व्रजेत् ॥ ३२ ॥
Manuscript NoteAI-assisted transcription
वृत्त: द्रुतविलम्बित। पाद ४: "श्वशुरसद्ममुखी" पढ़ा गया (हिन्दी: "ससुराल की ओर")। हिन्दी अनुवाद के अन्त में कोश-उद्धरण: "(सुषमा परमा शोभा) इत्यमरः"।
Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Drutavilambita. Pāda 4 is read as "श्वशुरसद्ममुखी" (the Hindi: "towards the in-laws' house"). The Hindi translation ends with a lexicon citation: "(सुषमा = परमा शोभा) इत्यमरः", "suṣamā means supreme beauty", says the Amarakośa.
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Among the horses, elephants and the rest, in their spaced-out lines, such a splendour was seen as if supreme beauty itself, in well-arrayed ranks, were then setting off towards the house of the father-in-law. (32)

English Translation (of the Hindi)AI-assisted translation
The look of the wedding guests on the well-decked horses and elephants is such that it seems as if supreme beauty itself, adorned and arrayed in rows, had set out towards the in-laws' house. (32) ("suṣamā" means supreme beauty, says the Amarakośa.)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
As the wedding procession was made ready, the lovely [unclear: white] line of horses on one side and the long, broad black line of the elephant-host on the other looked most charming, each apart. Seeing its wonderful, matchless splendour, it seemed as though Queen Suṣamā (Queen Beauty) herself, in person, were going to her own in-laws' house. That is, the indescribable splendour of that procession was unique. (32)
