English NotesAI-assisted translation
"Lord! I have often heard that two excellent sages (named Nara and Nārāyaṇa) have been living here practising austerity since endlessly ancient times, for countless days. I feel that you are those two sages. Today, merely by seeing you two, these two eyes of mine are blessed, I am fulfilled, and today my mind, greatly delighted, has blossomed with overflowing joy." (31)

English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
"The divine women, the apsarases of the dwellers in heaven, sent by Indra to destroy [your] austerity, met Urvaśī of the lovely thighs, whom [Nārāyaṇa] produced by splitting open his thigh, and went back to their own city, indignant." (32)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
"I have heard that the king of the gods is always afraid of the austerities of sages. So, frightened by the austerity of you both, Indra, king of the gods, sent Menakā and other divine apsarases to break your austerity. But Lord Nārāyaṇa brought out from his thigh a wonderful divine woman named Urvaśī and gave her to them; pained by her flawless beauty and sweetness, all the apsarases, full of indignation, took her with them and went to their city, Svargapurī." (32)
