English NotesAI-assisted translation
Before praising Śiva, the deity worshipped by my hero, the great sage Mārkaṇḍeya, who lives to the end of seven kalpas and attained long life to the end of seven kalpas, I bow from the heart, for the completion of [this work], to this group of seven long-lived ones: King Bali; the mighty Vāli; Hanumān, son of the Wind, a supremely steadfast brahmacārī; Vibhīṣaṇa, king of Laṅkā, single-minded devotee of Rāma; the teacher Droṇa's son Aśvatthāmā, a supremely daring, matchless warrior; the maharṣi and teacher Kṛpa, family teacher of the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas and eyewitness of the Mahābhārata war; and Paraśurāma, [unclear], single-mindedly devoted to his father: may they give me strength to fulfil my aim. (5)

Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Vaṃśastha. In pāda 1, "लब्धौ" is written over an earlier word. Pāda 2 reads "स्पृहा" (confirmed by the Hindi "इच्छा"), and pāda 4 "गदितुं मनो दधे"; the earlier reading "…कथां ततस्तां जीवेत्तु" was wrong.
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
My mind is not absorbed in gaining poetic skill, nor have I even a little longing to spread my fame; since one gains the highest immortality through remembrance, I have set my mind on telling that story. (6)

Notesgrandfather's hand
कवि अपने हृद्गत भावना द्वारा कवित्व-स्वाभिमान का परिहार करते हुए कहता है कि:— मैं एक विशिष्ट कवि के रूप में प्रख्यात होऊँ ऐसी भावना नहीं है। मुझे तनिक भी अपने यशः कामना की भी अभिलाषा नहीं, किन्तु स्मृति ही ऐसी वस्तु है जो अमरत्व प्राप्त करती है, अतः मेरे मन में एक ऐसे ईश्वरार्पित सप्तकल्पान्त जीवी, समाराधित मृत्युञ्जय देव के अहैतुकी कृपापात्र, चिरजीवियों में भी श्रेष्ठ महर्षि मार्कण्डेय की कथा कहने की अभिलाषा विवश करती है। ६
English NotesAI-assisted translation
Setting aside, through the feeling in his heart, the pride of being a poet, the poet says: I have no wish to become famous as a distinguished poet. I have not the least desire even for fame; but remembrance is the one thing that attains immortality. So a longing compels my mind to tell the story of Maharṣi Mārkaṇḍeya: devoted to God, living to the end of seven kalpas, recipient of the causeless grace of the god Mṛtyuñjaya whom he worshipped, the best even among the long-lived. (6)
