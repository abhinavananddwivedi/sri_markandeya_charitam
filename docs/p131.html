Verse 25
Shlokamanuensis's hand
अथ वरं शुभवेषविधानतः कनकयानगतं रुचिराननाः । युवतयो दधिलाजशिलासुत- भ्रमणपूर्वपरीक्षणमुज्जगुः ॥ २५ ॥

Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Drutavilambita. "शिलासुत" = the grinding-stone pestle (loṛhā), as the Hindi says. The last word of pāda 4 is read as "मुज्जगुः".
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Then fair-faced young women, with curd, parched grain and the circling of the grinding-stone, sang out the parīchan of the bridegroom, finely dressed and seated in a golden carriage. (25)

Fair-faced young women performed the parīchan of the bridegroom (dūlhā), finely dressed and seated in a golden palanquin, with curd and parched grain and by circling the grinding-stone (loṛhā). (25)

Manuscript NoteAI-assisted transcription
वृत्त: द्रुतविलम्बित। पाद ४: "योगपरायणाः" जैसा पढ़ा जाता है; हिन्दी "लौकिक कार्य में दक्ष" से "लोकपरायणाः" की सम्भावना है — दोनों छन्द में ठीक।

English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Making on his forehead a pure dark tilaka with musk, the well-wishing young women, devoted to [worldly custom], ah, guarded him against the crooked (evil) eye. (26)

The young women who wished the bridegroom well, skilled in worldly custom, put on his forehead a most beautiful [added above: tilaka and a] black mark of mṛgamada (musk) and guarded him from the evil eye. (26)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
On the occasion of the wedding procession, wishing him every good fortune, the young women put a most charming black mark made of musk on the bridegroom's lofty forehead (brow). Wishing for his lasting welfare, lest anyone's crooked (hostile) eye fall on him and harm him, they protected him [unclear: in every way]. (26)
