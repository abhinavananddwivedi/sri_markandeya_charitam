विभाकरालोकन दूय मानसा पुनर्बका लोडन जीर्णतन्त्वरा। जडात्मके भर्त्तरि सेर्ष्यया स्थिता कुमुद्वती तेन क्वचिद्विलोकिता ॥ ७ ॥
Manuscript NoteAI-assisted transcription
वृत्त: वंशस्थ। पाद ३ "सेर्ष्यया" (पूर्व "सेर्ष्याका")। पाद ४ में "क्व" से पूर्व ह्रस्व को लघु माना गया है (कवि की छूट)।
Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Vaṃśastha. Pāda 3 reads "सेर्ष्यया" (earlier "सेर्ष्याका"). In pāda 4 the short vowel before "क्व" is treated as light (the poet's licence).
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Somewhere he saw a night lotus, her mind pained at the sight of the sun, her slender stalk worn by the herons' stirring, standing with jealousy because her husband [the moon] was dull. (7)

English Translation (of the Hindi)AI-assisted translation
Again, somewhere he saw the night lotus (kumudvatī), her mind afflicted at seeing the sun, her leaves worn and torn by the herons' stirring, standing in jealousy because her husband had become dull (lustreless). (7)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
Then the sage Mṛkaṇḍu also saw a row of night-lotus (koī) flowers, crushed again and again by the constant trampling of the foolish herons, and lying very faint and limp at the sight of the sharp rays of Lord Bhāskara; because their husband the moon had become waned and lustreless, they lay senseless, very dim and full of jealousy. (7)

वृत्त: वंशस्थ (चारों पाद शुद्ध)। पाद ३ "सद्दलैः" (पूर्व "सदलैः")।
Manuscript Note (EN)AI-assisted transcription
Metre: Vaṃśastha (all four pādas scan). Pāda 3 reads "सद्दलैः" (earlier "सदलैः").
English Translation (of the Sanskrit)AI-assisted translation
Somewhere, in masses of water that had left the stream and lay in hollows, he saw a lotus plant in full bloom, of wonderful splendour, covered with fine leaves like emeralds. (8)

Again he saw, in the water standing in a hollow, having left the stream, a blooming lotus plant of wonderful splendour, covered with lovely leaves like emeralds. (8)

English NotesAI-assisted translation
Also, in places, where water had collected in small hollows, separated from the main stream, the sage Mṛkaṇḍu looked with great delight at the charming, delicate, blooming lotus plants in them, with green leaves of unequalled divine beauty that outshone even green emeralds of rare splendour. (8)
